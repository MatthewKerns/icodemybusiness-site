# Constraint Framework Reference

Detailed definitions of the 8 universal e-commerce constraint categories. Use when scoring a brand. Each entry: what it is, what failure looks like, symptoms, structural cause.

---

## 1. Capital / Cash Conversion Cycle

**What it is:** The cash conversion cycle (CCC) = days inventory outstanding + days sales outstanding − days payable outstanding. For imported FBA goods the cycle is long and brutal: pay the supplier → 30–60 days production → 30–45 days freight → days-to-weeks for the platform to receive and rank → then sales → then the platform disburses on a delay. Cash can be tied up 90–150 days before returning.

**Failure looks like:** Can't fund the next purchase order while waiting on the last one to sell through.

**Symptoms:** Reactive ordering, debt to bridge gaps, skipped reorders, stockouts caused by cash rather than demand.

**Structural cause:** Long CCC + thin margins + growth. In this model growth *consumes* cash rather than generating it.

---

## 2. Inventory

**What it is:** The twin failures of stockouts and overstock. Stockouts kill rank (the expensive-to-rebuild asset); overstock incurs storage fees and ties up cash. Lead times, MOQs, and seasonal factory closures compress the decision windows.

**Failure looks like:** Out of stock during peak demand, or aged inventory bleeding fees.

**Symptoms:** Rank drops, panic air-freight, liquidation, missed seasonal windows.

**Structural cause:** Forecasting under uncertainty with long lead times and lumpy minimum order quantities.

---

## 3. Channel

**What it is:** Platform dependency. When one marketplace is the only channel, the brand is exposed to TOS changes, account suspension, fee increases, and algorithm shifts it doesn't control.

**Failure looks like:** Account health warning, listing suppression, or a fee hike that erases margin.

**Symptoms:** Single point of failure; no owned customer relationship; a rented audience.

**Structural cause:** The convenience of platform-fulfilled traffic creates a dependency that is hard to unwind.

---

## 4. Brand & Audience

**What it is:** Whether there's emotional connection and an owned audience. Without them, every sale is a fresh paid acquisition. With them, retention rises, repeat purchase compounds, word-of-mouth lowers acquisition cost, and the brand owns the relationship instead of renting it.

**Failure looks like:** Commodity competition on price; no repeat purchase; rising ad costs.

**Symptoms:** Low brand-name search volume, no email list, reviews as the only social proof, listings that look generic.

**Structural cause:** Treating the product as a SKU rather than a brand; no post-purchase relationship. A good brand makes the customer *feel* something — its absence is a real constraint, not a soft one.

---

## 5. Operational (Task Volume)

**What it is:** E-commerce has many task categories — listing, PPC, inventory, sourcing, content, CS, finance, analytics. A solo operator is the single processor for all of them. The constraint isn't any one task; it's the aggregate volume against one person's hours.

**Failure looks like:** Important-but-not-urgent work (R&D, brand building) perpetually deferred.

**Symptoms:** Reactive firefighting, carry-over tasks, strategy that never gets executed. The classic tell: "when I focus on one area for a week, everything else falls apart."

**Structural cause:** No team, no systems/SOPs, the founder as the bottleneck for every decision. This is *frequently the true binding constraint* hiding behind complaints about marketing or branding.

---

## 6. Capability

**What it is:** Skill and knowledge gaps. Distinct from task volume — this is whether the operator knows *how*, not whether they have *time*.

**Failure looks like:** Tasks done poorly or avoided due to lack of skill.

**Symptoms:** Outsourcing things that should be core, repeated mistakes, tools bought and underused.

**Structural cause:** E-commerce demands a wide skill range few individuals fully possess. But beware over-diagnosing this — a technically capable founder's problem is usually bandwidth (operational), not capability.

---

## 7. Market

**What it is:** Competitive intensity, the category demand ceiling, and seasonality. Some constraints are external and can only be navigated, not solved.

**Failure looks like:** Race to the bottom on price; demand caps growth regardless of execution.

**Symptoms:** Shrinking margins, undifferentiated listings, commoditization.

**Structural cause:** Low barriers to entry in many categories invite competition.

---

## 8. Regulatory / Structural

**What it is:** Tariffs, import duties, TOS compliance, IP/trademark, and business entity structure. Usually stable but capable of sudden severe impact (e.g. a tariff change on imported goods).

**Failure looks like:** A tariff or policy change that erases margin overnight.

**Symptoms:** Margin sensitivity to duty rates, trademark disputes, compliance gaps.

**Structural cause:** Dependency on imported goods plus platform rules outside the brand's control.

---

## Scoring guidance

- Be honest about severity. If everything scores Critical, either it's a genuine crisis or the scoring is inflated — usually the latter.
- Trajectory matters as much as severity. A worsening Medium deserves more attention than a stable High.
- Leverage is the practical filter: a Critical constraint with Low leverage (e.g. a tariff) can only be monitored, while a High constraint with High leverage is where to spend energy.
- Exactly one constraint should be binding in most cases. If you can't pick one, look for the two that co-bind and reinforce each other.
