# Opportunity Scoring Rubric

Detailed scoring guidance for the four prioritization lenses. Use alongside the opportunity-prioritization skill.

---

## Lens 1: OBG-alignment

The One Big Goal is the north star, and it is **discovered from the owner, not assumed.** It might be profitability, growth, scale, a successful launch, owner time-freedom, or an exit. Do not default to a profitability goal — a brand may already be profitable and want to grow, or want time back. Use the brand's *stated* OBG. If it genuinely hasn't been stated yet, that's a data gap to close before ranking, not a blank to fill with a guess.

- **High** — directly moves the stated OBG. (If the goal is margin: improves margin on existing products or advances a validated new product. If the goal is growth: drives new revenue or audience. If the goal is time-freedom: removes owner hours.)
- **Medium** — supports the OBG indirectly (e.g. infrastructure that enables the goal, owned-audience building that lowers future CAC).
- **Low** — doesn't clearly serve the stated OBG. Default to deferral unless it's critical infrastructure (e.g. fixing a broken email capture) or a genuine emergency.

The test: "Does this move the goal the owner actually stated? If it serves a goal they didn't name, question whether it belongs on the priority list right now."

---

## Lens 2: The 4L Filter

Score each of the four dimensions. Favor opportunities that are Low on all four — they're the ones a constrained operator can actually execute.

- **Low Human** — minimal human labor / headcount to run. (AI agents and automations score well; anything needing a hire scores poorly.)
- **Low Capital** — minimal cash to start. (Critical for cash-constrained brands — a great idea that needs capital the brand doesn't have is not actionable yet.)
- **Low Complexity** — few moving parts, easy to maintain. (Complexity is the enemy of execution.)
- **Low Tech** — doesn't require building or maintaining sophisticated technology. (Though for a technical founder, "low tech" bends — building is cheap when you have the skill.)

An opportunity that's High on multiple L's isn't disqualified, but it needs a correspondingly higher impact to justify the cost, and it probably belongs later in the sequence.

---

## Lens 3: Impact / Effort

A 2x2. Impact and Effort each Low / Medium / High.

- **High impact / Low effort** = quick win. These go to the top. Especially if they also address the binding constraint.
- **High impact / High effort** = strategic bet. Worth doing, but slot with a realistic timeline and don't try to do several at once.
- **Low impact / Low effort** = filler. Do only if trivial and adjacent to other work.
- **Low impact / High effort** = kill. These are where time goes to die.

Be honest about effort. Founders systematically underestimate it. A "quick" listing overhaul that requires new photography, copy, and A+ content is not low effort.

---

## Lens 4: Platform-risk / White-space test

Mainly for tooling, automation, and "build something" opportunities. The question: will a platform subsume this before it pays off?

- **Defensible white space** — a platform is unlikely to build this; it requires brand-specific custom context; it complements rather than competes with the big players. Favor these.
- **At-risk** — a marketplace feature, a general AI assistant, or an established platform tool is likely to do this natively soon. Building it is a race you may lose.
- **Trap** — high-effort AND at-risk. The worst quadrant: months of work that gets eaten. Kill these unless there's a clear, defensible angle.

This test exists because it's easy to spend months building something that a platform releases for free six months later. The defensible play is usually narrow, custom, and complementary — not a broad horizontal tool competing with well-funded platforms.

---

## Combining the lenses

No formula — judgment. But the pattern for a **P0/P1 quick win** is consistent:
- Addresses the binding constraint, AND
- High impact / Low effort, AND
- Low on most 4L dimensions, AND
- High or Medium OBG fit.

The pattern for a **kill-list item**:
- Low impact / High effort, OR
- Fails the platform-risk test (high-effort + at-risk), OR
- Doesn't address the binding constraint and isn't a quick win, OR
- Has been "about to happen" for months — a sign the constraint is time/attention, which more tooling won't fix.

When two opportunities tie, prefer the one that addresses the binding constraint. That's the whole point of doing the constraint map first.
