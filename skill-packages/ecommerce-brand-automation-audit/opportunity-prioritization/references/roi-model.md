# ROI Model — Ecommerce (Path A / Path B)

ROI is only as honest as its baseline. The qualifying question, asked once in intake, sets the path:

> "Do you know your CM3 / your daily operating cost off the top of your head?"

## Path A — they don't know (most owners)

The discovery *is* the value — you're helping them see their own business. Don't penalize it; normalize it ("totally normal, most owners don't track this"). Build the baseline together: help them gather time-per-task and cost data, populate the inputs collaboratively, and let the ROI emerge from their own numbers as they fill them in. Until then, every opportunity's ROI stays `"TBD until baseline provided"`. A plan built on invented economics is worse than one honest about its gaps.

## Path B — they know their numbers (rare)

Acknowledge it ("that's rare — means we can move faster"), collect the metrics, validate quickly, and attach real ROI to each opportunity with the inputs used.

## Ecommerce inputs

Labor: hourly cost per role (salary ÷ 2,080), hours/week per task (tracked or flagged estimate). Revenue: AOV, monthly orders/units, conversion rate. Cost: CM1/CM2/CM3, landed cost/unit, ad spend, daily operating cost. Waste: lost revenue from stockouts/abandoned carts/returns, time on low-value repetitive tasks. Time: how fast they want it, cost of each week of delay.

## Formulas

```
Current annual cost = (labor hrs × hourly rate) + waste + lost revenue + overhead
Target annual cost  = reduced labor + reduced waste + captured revenue + solution cost
Annual savings      = current − target
ROI %               = (annual savings − investment) ÷ investment × 100
Payback (months)    = investment ÷ monthly savings
```

Show conservative / moderate / aggressive scenarios rather than a single point estimate.

## Transparency model

Share the calculator; don't hide it. The owner fills in their own numbers and can adjust any input. They own the accuracy; you provide the framework. As they refine, confidence climbs from rough to real. This removes the "where are you getting these numbers?" objection entirely.

## Anti-patterns

Don't guess baselines — get real numbers or mark unknown. Don't promise ROI without data. Don't skip the qualifier — it sets the whole path. Don't build before baselining — without metrics there's no way to measure success. Every ROI figure traces to an owner-provided baseline; if it doesn't, it's "TBD," not a number.
