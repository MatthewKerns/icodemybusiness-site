# 5 Questions Framework — Ecommerce

The spine of intake. Ask in order. Listen more than talk. Their answers become the plan. Top-level questions never change; the drill-downs get filled with the owner's prior answers (especially Q1). Don't propose until all five are asked. Works at either pace — Quick Pass takes the short version of each; Deep Dive lets the owner run and you drill with the 11-category bank.

---

## Q1 — What's the biggest problem in the business right now?

Phrasings: "What's the biggest challenge right now?" · "What's taking the most time day-to-day?" · "As you grow, what's the bottleneck slowing you down?"

For ecommerce, anchor on the leaking funnel stage. Intent: surface the single biggest pain in their words — the spine of everything that follows.

Listen for: the recurring bottleneck; manual/repetitive work; a funnel stage that's broken (traffic, conversion, fulfillment, retention); what they raise first, unprompted. Green light: a specific, real problem. Red flag: "no real problems" — probe, because no pain means no plan.

## Q2 — How much is that costing per month?

Phrasings: "Roughly how much revenue are you leaving on the table each month because of [Q1]?" · "How much of your time goes to things that could be systematized?" · "What's the cost of [stockouts / abandoned carts / manual reporting]?"

Intent: quantify in dollars (or time converted to dollars). Reveals whether they actually value solving it, and sets the budget ceiling. Quick Pass: take a flagged guess. Path A (doesn't know the numbers): "totally normal — building that baseline is part of the value," leave it TBD. Don't invent a figure.

Listen for: dollar amounts even rough; time waste; leaked/lost revenue; rework.

## Q3 — How long have you had this problem?

Phrasings: "How long has this been going on?" · "Tried anything to fix it?" · "What's stopped you solving it before now?" · "Getting better, worse, or flat?"

Intent: chronicity and history. New or chronic? Tried and failed? Reveals how entrenched it is. Red flag: "tried everything" — change-resistant, probe gently.

## Q4 — If you do nothing, what do the next 6–12 months look like?

Phrasings: "If nothing changes, where's the business in a year?" · "What does this do to your ability to scale?" · "What does it mean for rank / retention / cash?"

Intent: cost of inaction — the stakes that justify acting now. Listen for: growth ceiling, burnout, churn, competitive disadvantage, cash crunch, missed seasonal windows. Urgent tone is a green light.

## Q5 — If you had a magic wand, what would the perfect solution look like?

Phrasings: "If the perfect system were in place and [Q1] were fully solved, what would day-to-day look like?" · "What would you be doing instead of [manual task]?" · "What does success look like 90 days out?"

Intent: their ideal outcome, success metrics, must-haves, dealbreakers — in their words. This defines what to propose. Excited and detailed is a green light; vague means they don't yet know what they want — probe.

---

## Facilitation

Listen 70%, talk 30%. Ask in order. Use THEIR words in the recap. Push past surface answers; push every pain past the current product. Going off-track → "that's helpful — let me ask…". Price asked too early → "we'll get there — first help me understand…". Running out of time → skip to vision and close.

### The recap (play all five back, in order)
> "So if I've got this right: [Q1 problem] is costing roughly [Q2] a month, you've dealt with it for [Q3], if nothing changes [Q4], and the ideal would look like [Q5]. Did I get that right?"

After Q1–Q5 you know: the problem, what they'll pay/value, how urgent, why act now, and exactly what to propose. Their answers ARE the plan.
